Two galaxies, gravity, and nothing else.

Everything in this film is a live simulation: 300,000 massless test particles
orbiting analytic potentials on the GPU, rendered at 4K and 60 frames per second.
No animation, no artistic licence, no post-processing of the physics. The
parameters used to produce each scene are shown on screen as it begins.

You can run all of it yourself, in your browser, right now:
https://galaxies.gillett-projects.com

Source, the full build log, and an honest account of what it gets wrong:
https://github.com/tonygillett136/galaxies

Commentary is on the subtitle track. Turn it on with the CC button, or leave it
off and just watch.


WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

This is a restricted three-body model. Massless test particles move in rigid
analytic potentials, the two galaxies attract each other, and the particles feel
both galaxies but not one another. It is the approximation Alar and Juri Toomre
used in 1972 to show that the long bridges and tails around interacting galaxies
are tidal, rather than magnetic or explosive.

That approximation buys enormous speed. It also leaves things out, and the film
says so: no gas, no star formation, no disc self-gravity. Nothing here is a fluid.

Distances are in kiloparsecs, about 3,260 light years. Times are in millions of
years, measured from closest approach.


THE RESULT WORTH WATCHING FOR

Compare the prograde and retrograde passages at 0:25 and 1:11. Same masses, same
orbit, same closest approach. The only difference is which way the discs spin.

Prograde throws 15.1 per cent of a disc beyond 20 kiloparsecs. Retrograde throws
2.5 per cent. The two orbits are asserted identical to one part in a million
first, so the disc spin is demonstrably the only difference.

Which way a galaxy spins matters more than how close the encounter is.


ON THE NUMBERS

Every figure quoted in the commentary is measured by the project's own test
suite and registered, so the prose fails the build if it drifts from the
measurement that produced it. The merger timings, the ring density, the tidal
fractions and the disc stability check are generated rather than typed.

The one thing this project will not claim is that a good visual match means you
have found the right encounter. It published exactly that claim, measured it
properly, and retracted it. The reasoning is in the repository.
